Overarching Research Question:
How does generative AI influence the design autonomy and skill development of novice interaction designers in a university prototyping course (IDD)?

Sub-questions:

RQ1 — Decisional Autonomy:
When AI generates most design outputs, do students' design decisions reflect their authentic desires and visions, or do they predominantly accept AI's path of least resistance?
- Are choices grounded in the student's own design intentions (internalist authenticity)?
- Or do choices primarily reflect what AI makes easy/possible (externalist — wrong causal history)?

RQ2 — Practical Autonomy:
How do students recapture practical control when AI's technical capabilities exceed their own?
- What strategies do students use to transform AI outputs into "their" designs?
- When does "editing down AI slop" constitute genuine practical agency versus passive curation?

RQ3 — Technical Competence & Skill Development:
How does AI-mediated design affect students' development of foundational skills and ability to articulate design rationale?

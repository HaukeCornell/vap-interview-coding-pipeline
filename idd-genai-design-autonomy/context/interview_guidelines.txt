Interview Protocol: IDD GenAI Study (60 min)
Post-class interviews with students from Cornell Tech's Interaction Design course (IDD), conducted after final grades were submitted.

Context: Students built interactive devices across 6 labs + a final project. They used AI tools (primarily Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor/Copilot) throughout. All grading is complete; students were told their answers cannot affect academic standing.

--- SECTION 1: Setup (5 min) ---
- Confirm consent and recording
- Ask participant to open final project and any AI chat logs on screen

--- SECTION 2: Final Project Baseline (8 min) ---
- Walk me through your final project. What were you originally trying to make?
- Why did you want to design this? How did you come up with the idea?
- Looking at what you actually built — is this what you envisioned, or did it become something else?
- If you had a bit more time, skills, and resources, would your project look different?
- Roughly, how much of the final design/implementation came from your ideas vs. AI suggestions?

--- SECTION 3: Decisional Autonomy — High AI Influence (12 min) ---
- Show me a project or lab where AI had the MOST influence on design decisions (not just output).
- How did this happen? What did you ask AI for? What did it give you?
- Did you go with AI's version because you thought it was better, or because it was easier/faster?
- How did you iterate with AI? Did you prompt for specific values (fun, creative, minimal)?
- If AI hadn't suggested this, would you have made something different?
- When do you decide the implementation is "good enough"?

--- SECTION 3B: Decisional Autonomy — Low AI Influence (contrast) ---
- Do you have an example where AI suggested something and you rejected it?
- Show me something where AI had LESS influence — where the final design is closer to what you originally wanted.
- Why was AI less influential here? Walk me through going from AI output to what you actually wanted.

--- SECTION 4: Practical Autonomy (15 min) ---
- Can you explain how [high AI influence example] actually works — like if I'm not a programmer?
- If I took away AI right now and asked you to rebuild [feature], could you? Where would you get stuck?
- Were there moments where you felt "I have no idea what I'm really doing but it's working"?
  → If yes: Tell me about that. Did it bother you?

--- SECTION 5: Editing Process (8 min) ---
- Did you experience generating something with AI and then having to edit it down significantly?
  → If yes: Show me an example.
- When you were editing — were you shaping it toward YOUR vision, or making AI's output work?
- At what point did it feel like it became "yours"?

--- SECTION 6: Authorship (8 min) ---
- Do you feel like you DESIGNED this project, or ASSEMBLED it from AI and other pieces?
- If you got $1,000 for this project, how would you split it between: you, teammates, AI?
- Why did you give AI that amount?

--- SECTION 7: Reflection (4 min) ---
- Looking back, what did you learn from this class that you'll actually remember?
- If you taught IDD next year with AI, what would you tell students to do differently?
- Anything about AI and design we didn't cover?

--- What Each Section Targets ---
Sections 3/3B → Decisional autonomy (did AI determine the outcome?)
Section 4    → Practical autonomy (can they execute without AI?)
Section 5    → Practical autonomy / editing (how do they recapture control?)
Section 6    → Authorship and legitimacy
Section 7    → Skill development and meta-reflection

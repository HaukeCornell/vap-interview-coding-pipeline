Theoretical Lens: Pugh's Two-Dimensional Framework of Autonomy
Source: Pugh, J. (2020). Autonomy, Rationality, and Contemporary Bioethics. NBK556861.

The framework treats autonomy as having two primary dimensions — Decisional and Practical —
each with distinct sub-elements. Applied here to student design agency in AI-mediated IDD work.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DIMENSION 1: DECISIONAL AUTONOMY
Whether the agent is making their own decisions about what to do.
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Cognitive Element — pertaining to the agent's beliefs and understanding
  Agents must act "with understanding" — they cannot form autonomous choices without
  knowing what the options available to them are like.
  → In IDD: Does the student understand what the AI is generating and why? Can they
    articulate the design rationale? Do they know what alternatives exist?

Reflective Element — pertaining to the agent's reflective endorsement of their motivating desire
  Addresses the authenticity of desires — whether decisions align with the agent's own
  endorsed values rather than "impostor desires" that feel alien or externally imposed.
  A properly reflective agent is "reflectively autonomous": their motivating desires are
  genuinely their own (not distorted by addiction, manipulation, or external conditioning).
  → In IDD: Did the student adopt AI's design direction because they genuinely preferred
    it, or because it was the default / path of least resistance? Is the design vision
    theirs, or the AI's absorbed without critical reflection?

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DIMENSION 2: PRACTICAL AUTONOMY
Whether the agent can act on the basis of their decisions.
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Negative Freedom — freedom from debilitating forces that prevent the agent from acting
  A negative obligation not to restrain the autonomous actions of others; absence of
  interference, coercion, or debilitating constraints.
  → In IDD: Is the student constrained by AI tool limitations, skill gaps, or time pressure
    in ways that prevent them from executing their design intentions?

Positive Freedom — freedom afforded by possession of certain abilities and capacities for action
  A positive obligation to facilitate an agent's ability to act autonomously; requires
  actual competence to translate decisions into action.
  → In IDD: Does the student possess (or develop) the skills to implement their design
    vision independently? Can they rebuild without AI? Do they understand enough to
    meaningfully direct AI rather than passively accept output?

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SCOPE: LOCAL vs. GLOBAL AUTONOMY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Local autonomy — specific decisions at particular moments
  → Applies to individual lab/project choices: did AI determine this feature?

Global autonomy — a whole way of designing, assessed across the course
  → Applies across all labs: does the student's overall design approach reflect their
    own evolving values and intentions, or a consistent deference to AI defaults?

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
APPLICATION TO CODING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

RQ1 (Decisional) maps to both elements of Dimension 1:
  - Cognitive: Can the student explain/articulate the AI-generated work?
  - Reflective: Did they genuinely endorse it, or absorb it by default?

RQ2 (Practical) maps to Dimension 2:
  - Negative freedom: What constraints prevented executing their vision?
  - Positive freedom: What capabilities did or didn't they have to act on their decisions?

RQ3 (Skill development) maps to the Positive Freedom element over time (global scope):
  - Is AI building or eroding the capacities needed for autonomous design action?

# Research Questions — comparative (CHI 2027, two-cohort study)

**This file governs the early-ai (Fall'23) coding pass and the CHI paper.**
`context/research_question.txt` is preserved unchanged as the historical record of what the
vibe-ai (Fall'25) coders were actually given. Do not edit that file.

## Why the revision is deliberately small

The vibe-ai wave was coded under RQ1–RQ3 as originally written. If the early-ai wave is coded
under materially different questions, the two arms are no longer comparable and the recode
loses its purpose. So RQ1–RQ3 keep their substance and their autonomy framing. Two changes
were unavoidable, and one question is added.

**Fix 1 — RQ1's premise was cohort-specific.** The original opens *"When AI generates most
design outputs\ldots"*. That was true of the Fall'25 cohort and false of Fall'23, where AI
produced fragments a student pasted in by hand. Handed to an early-ai coder it imports a
presupposition the data will not support, and invites either forced codes or spurious absence.
Rewritten to be neutral about how much AI produced.

**Fix 2 — RQ2 presupposed its own answer.** *"How do students recapture practical control"*
assumes recapture occurs. For a comparison this is leading in exactly the wrong direction: one
plausible early-ai finding is that there was less to recapture. Rewritten as whether and how.

**Addition — RQ4.** The comparative question has no equivalent in the original set, because
there was nothing to compare against when it was written.

## Overarching

How does generative AI shape the design autonomy and skill development of novice interaction
designers in a university prototyping course — and how has that changed as AI's reach extended
from execution into discovery and reflection?

## RQ1 — Decisional Autonomy

Whose design intentions do students' decisions express, and what do students understand about
what was produced on their behalf?

- **Cognitive element.** Can the student explain what was built and why? Do they know what
  alternatives existed? Where did the design diverge from their intention, and do they know it
  diverged?
- **Reflective element.** Are choices grounded in the student's own design intentions
  (internalist authenticity), or do they reflect what AI made easy or default (externalist —
  wrong causal history)?
- Where did the concept originate, and how does the student account for that origin?

## RQ2 — Practical Autonomy

Whether and how students act on their own design decisions when AI's technical capabilities
exceed their own.

- **Negative freedom.** What constrained the student from executing their intention — tool
  limits, skill gaps, time, incomprehension of AI-produced work?
- **Positive freedom.** What capacities let them direct AI rather than accept its output, and
  where did those capacities come from?
- When does transforming AI output constitute genuine agency, and when is it passive curation?

## RQ3 — Technical Competence and Skill Development

How does AI-mediated design affect students' development of foundational skills and their
ability to articulate design rationale?

- Is AI building or eroding the capacities that autonomous design action requires?
- For students with no prior competence, are the conditions for acquiring it present at all?

## RQ4 — Change Across Cohorts *(new)*

Which autonomy patterns persist across the two cohorts, which intensify, and which appear only
once AI reached discovery and reflection?

- Which phenomena are **absent in early-ai because the tooling could not produce them** — as
  distinct from absent because students behaved differently? The capability tiering in
  `state_2023/code_system_2023.json` was fixed in advance precisely so this distinction can be
  defended rather than asserted.
- Which patterns run in the **opposite** direction to expectation — stronger in early-ai than
  in vibe-ai? Output homogenisation and the search-versus-generate equivalence are the two
  candidates flagged before coding.

## Cross-cutting instruction for coders

For every RQ, attend to **conditions, not just outcomes**. The study's central finding in the
vibe-ai wave is that autonomy is *governance-contingent* — students hold it to the degree that
they arrived with a design conviction firm enough to direct the tool. So wherever a student
retains or loses control, the analytically valuable material is what made the difference:
prior skill, strength of the design idea, motivation, stakes, domain, team role.

## Scope: local vs global autonomy

- **Local** — specific decisions at particular moments (did AI determine this feature?).
- **Global** — a whole way of designing across the course (does the student's overall approach
  reflect their own evolving values, or consistent deference to AI defaults?).

Group interviews make local claims easier to attribute than global ones. Record which you have.
